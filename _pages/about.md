I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of Luxembourg and a doctoral researcher at the Luxembourg Institute of Socio-Economic Research (LISER). My research lies at the intersection of labour economics, public economics, social choice and welfare economics.

I develop structural discrete-choice labour-supply models to study how heterogeneous job opportunities and preferences shape individual choices and money-metric well-being inequality. My main empirical project models labour supply as a choice among latent job packages and quantifies the model-based contributions of access to jobs, earning capacity, and preferences to inequality in well-being.

My other work develops the axiomatic foundations of well-being measurement when people choose among jobs rather than continuous working hours, and uses EUROMOD microsimulation to evaluate the distributional effects of tax-benefit reforms.

## Job Market Paper

### Unequal Job Opportunities and Well-Being Inequality: A Latent-Jobs Structural Decomposition

This paper develops and estimates a structural labour-supply model in which individuals choose among latent job packages and face heterogeneous opportunity environments. It constructs money-metric measures of well-being and studies how inequality is attributable to access to jobs, ability or wage technology, and preferences.
