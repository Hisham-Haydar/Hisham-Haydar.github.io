## Working Papers and Research Projects

### Unequal Job Opportunities and Well-Being Inequality: A Latent-Jobs Structural Decomposition

**Job Market Paper - work in progress.**

This paper develops and estimates a structural discrete-choice labour-supply model in which individuals choose among latent job packages and face heterogeneous job opportunities. It distinguishes preferences over consumption and job characteristics from the opportunity environment governing access to jobs, hours arrangements, occupations, and wage prospects.

The paper constructs money-metric measures of individual well-being and decomposes inequality in well-being into components associated with job access, earning capacity or wage technology, and preferences. Its central question is how much money-metric well-being inequality is attributable to unequal opportunities rather than preference heterogeneity, and how much opportunity-related inequality is absorbed into estimated preferences when labour-supply models impose a common choice set.

### Jobs and Well-Being Measurement

**Joint with François Maniquet - work in progress.**

This paper asks how individual well-being should be measured when labour supply is represented as a choice among jobs rather than as a continuous choice of working time at an exogenous wage. It bridges discrete-choice labour-supply models and income-tax theory by separating the classical wage into two objects: the individual's ability set, which determines the jobs the person can perform, and the income profile, which specifies how much each job pays.

Within this framework, the paper revisits the normative conflict between compensation for unequal productive circumstances and responsibility for preference-based choices. It introduces axioms specific to jobs, including the treatment of irrelevant jobs, infeasible jobs, job duplication, and job neutrality; establishes new impossibility results; and characterises a family of money-metric well-being measures corresponding to different compromises between compensation and responsibility.

### Decomposing Indexation and Tax Reform: Evidence from Luxembourg's 2025 Tax-Benefit Package

**Joint with Nizamul Islam - work in progress.**

Using EUROMOD and Luxembourg EU-SILC data, this paper evaluates Luxembourg's 2025 tax-benefit package by separating three concurrent policy channels: automatic wage and benefit indexation, the personal-income-tax bracket reform, and other reforms including the energy allowance and REVIS/RPGH uprating. Eight counterfactual policy environments and an exact Shapley decomposition identify the contribution of each channel to disposable-income gains, poverty, inequality, and the protection of households that would otherwise lose.

The analysis is a static microsimulation accounting exercise under fixed behaviour. It shows that the channel driving mean disposable-income gains is not the channel driving poverty reduction: indexation dominates average gains, while targeted other reforms make the largest contribution to poverty reduction and to rescuing households that lose under indexation alone.
