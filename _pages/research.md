My research combines structural labour economics, public economics, social choice theory, and welfare measurement. I am currently working on three papers.

## Job Market Paper

### Unequal Job Opportunities and Well-Being Inequality: A Latent-Jobs Structural Decomposition

**Solo-authored · Work in progress**

This paper develops and estimates a structural discrete-choice labour-supply model in which individuals choose among latent job packages and face heterogeneous job opportunities. It distinguishes preferences over consumption and job characteristics from the opportunity environment governing which jobs, hours arrangements, occupations, and wage prospects are available.

The paper then constructs money-metric measures of individual well-being and decomposes inequality in well-being into components associated with access to jobs, earning capacity or wage technology, and preferences. Its central question is how much observed money-metric well-being inequality is attributable to unequal opportunities rather than preference heterogeneity, and how much opportunity-related inequality may be absorbed into estimated preferences when labour-supply models assume a common choice set.

## Other Working Papers

### Jobs and Well-Being Measurement

**With François Maniquet · Work in progress**

This paper asks how the measurement of individual well-being changes when labour supply is represented as a choice among jobs rather than as a continuous choice of working time at an exogenously given wage.

We bridge discrete-choice labour-supply models and income-tax theory by introducing jobs directly into the theoretical framework. The classical wage is separated into two objects: the individual’s ability set—the jobs the person can perform—and the income profile specifying what each job pays. Within this framework, we revisit the conflict between compensation for unequal productive circumstances and responsibility for preference-based choices, introduce axioms specific to jobs, establish impossibility results, and characterise a family of money-metric well-being measures.

### Decomposing Indexation and Tax Reform: Evidence from Luxembourg’s 2025 Tax-Benefit Package

**With Nizamul Islam · Work in progress**

This paper evaluates Luxembourg’s 2025 tax-benefit package using EUROMOD and Luxembourg EU-SILC data. It separates three concurrent policy channels—automatic indexation, the personal-income-tax bracket reform, and a bundle of other reforms including the energy allowance and REVIS/RPGH uprating—across eight counterfactual policy environments.

An exact Shapley decomposition identifies the contribution of each channel to disposable-income gains, poverty, inequality, and the protection of households that would lose under indexation alone. The analysis is a static microsimulation accounting exercise under fixed pre-reform behaviour; it does not estimate behavioural or general-equilibrium responses.
